How to read this, and the limits

  • Meters: hourly PM2.5 from the Delhi ground stations in the OpenAQ archive (CPCB, DPCC, IMD). "City mean" is the mean of all meters that report in that hour. The hour label can be off by 1 hour, because the sources do not say if a label is the start or the end of the hour.
  • Meter sources differ by year. 2025: about 40 Delhi meters, full values. 2024: 38 meters, but the source has no readings of 1000 or more, so we show those hours as 1000. 2022 and 2023: one meter only (US Embassy), with gaps. Faulty readings: we drop a reading when one meter is at 8 times the city median or more.
  • AQI: the Indian AQI stops at 500. It uses a 24 hour mean. A number such as 1000 or 3000 is not an Indian AQI value. It is an hourly PM2.5 reading, or a number from an app that uses a different scale.
  • Fires: NASA VIIRS (S-NPP satellite, 375 m). The satellite passes about 13:30 and 01:30. It does not see fires under cloud or thick smoke, and it does not see fires that start after it passes. Reports say that farmers now burn later in the day, so counts in recent years can be too low.
  • 2026 uses a different fire satellite. S-NPP sent no data after 2 Nov 2026. So 2026 uses NOAA-20, which has the same VIIRS instrument and passes about 50 minutes apart. Check for the 2025 season: NOAA-20 saw 24,033 fires and S-NPP saw 23,404. The season totals agree within 3%, but one day can differ a lot. The 2026 data is near real time and has no fire type, so a few factory heat sources (about 3% in 2025) are in the count. The answer cards above use full seasons only.
  • Wind: ERA5 reanalysis, 100 m above ground. The last 5 days or so of a season in progress come from a weather forecast model. Path of Delhi's air: we walk the air backward for 48 hours with this wind. It is a simple, one-level estimate. Fires on the path: fires seen that day within about 40 km of the path.
  • Heat layer: CAMS global model (about 45 km cells). It is a model, not a meter. It does not know about fireworks, so it does not show the Diwali night spike. This is useful: the spike that only the meters show is the cracker part.
  • NASA photo: true colour picture from VIIRS for that day (about 13:30). NASA haze map: MODIS aerosol optical depth, which is how much dust and smoke is in the full column of air. Gaps are cloud or no pass.
  • Rank link: Spearman rank correlation of the day values. +1 means the two go up together. 0 means no link. -1 means one goes down when the other goes up. Diwali day and the day after are left out.
  • Correlation is not proof. Cold, still nights and many fires come at the same time of year. The numbers here show what fits the data. They do not split the blame to an exact percent.

Sources

  • Fires: NASA FIRMS country archive, VIIRS S-NPP. Daily photo: NASA GIBS.
  • Meters: OpenAQ open data archive on AWS.
  • Wind and model PM2.5: Open-Meteo (ERA5 and CAMS, Copernicus).
